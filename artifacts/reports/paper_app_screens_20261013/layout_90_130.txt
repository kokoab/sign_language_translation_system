        ATLAS is a mobile application that recognizes a selected 100-sign ASL

vocabulary and produces English text and local speech from accepted gloss

sequences. The prototype connects camera input, visual feature extraction, sign

recognition, temporal boundary detection, sequence decoding and English generation

within one application. The following subsections describe the equipment, selected

processing components and user-facing operation of the prototype.

4.3.2 Final Models and Processing Components

        Following model development and evaluation, ATLAS integrated the selected

recognition, boundary-detection and English-generation components into a mobile

application. Each component performs a distinct task within the pipeline.

        Visual extraction. The landmark extractor obtains structured hand, face and

upper-body points from device-camera images. MobileCLIP2-S0 encodes cropped

hand images to supply complementary appearance features.

        Sign recognition. The final recognizer combines an adapted part-wise and

global Squeezeformer landmark branch with a temporal Squeezeformer hand-image

branch through a learned fusion classifier. The selected recognizer incorporates

interval fine-tuning with partial-sign and rejection examples. Its primary output

supplies the reported 95.50% isolated-sign validation accuracy, with transition-refined

evidence used selectively for evaluated sign transitions.

        Boundary detection. A separate four-layer Transformer predicts whether a

frame is outside a sign, at its beginning or within it. It was trained through knowledge

distillation from the frozen Sign Language Segmentation teacher. The student

operates on landmark features and provides temporal evidence for candidate sign

intervals; it does not determine the sign's vocabulary label.

        Sequence decoding and gloss management. The segmental decoder combines

boundary estimates, recognition scores and movement evidence to select and accept

signs. Ordering and acceptance rules support the resulting gloss sequence, which is

maintained in the visible gloss buffer. These processing rules are distinct from the

trained recognition and boundary models.

4.4.1 Implementation Overview


          The implementation plan concerns ATLAS: A Squeezeformer-Based System

conversion, connect the processing components correctly, provide the intended

application functions and document performance under the stated evaluation

        Combined operation of the deployed system. Camera frames supply landmark

and hand-image information. Boundary estimates guide candidate-interval selection,

and the recognizer scores those intervals using both visual inputs. The segmental

decoder selects the accepted sequence, Gloss Management preserves its order, and

T5-efficient-tiny prepares English as that sequence grows. Finish finalizes the

remaining text for display and local speech. The models therefore cooperate within

one workflow, while each retains a specific input, output and responsibility.

4.5.5 Functional Testing and Software Quality Evaluation


Distinction Between Technical System Testing and Human Participant
Evaluation
        The evaluation of ATLAS shall consist of two distinct components: (1)

technical system evaluation and (2) human participant evaluation. These components

shall be treated separately to distinguish system performance measurements from

information obtained directly from research participants.

        Technical system evaluation refers to the assessment of the technical and

functional performance of the developed system. This evaluation does not require

human participants and shall be conducted using prepared datasets, sign-sequence

data, gloss-to-English pairs, trained and exported models, controlled system inputs,

and other development or testing resources. Technical evaluation shall include

appropriate measures of system performance, such as Top-1 accuracy, Top-5 accuracy,

Word Error Rate (WER), English-output performance, processing time, and model

size, as applicable to the developed system.

        Human participant evaluation, on the other hand, refers to information

obtained directly from eligible research participants during the evaluation of the

system. Participant-provided information may include their responses to the

evaluation procedures, observations, comments, suggestions, and other feedback

collected through the approved research process.

        The technical performance results generated from datasets and system testing

shall not be treated as participant data. Conversely, information directly provided by

research participants shall be treated as research data and shall be handled in

accordance with the informed consent process, data protection safeguards, and the

approved research protocol.

Software Testing Results and Discussion

        Automated software testing examined the internal processing logic and

application behavior of ATLAS. The reported application subset comprised 47 distinct

cases: 38 internal-logic cases and nine interface and integration cases. All 47 passed

in their final recorded executions, with no failed or skipped cases remaining in the

reported subset. Results were recorded on October 13, 2026, and grouped by

component rather than programming language.

        The subset was drawn from checks of the documented processing and

application functions. The broader Python regression run also passed all 245 tests, but

its additional experimental-model, data-preparation and development checks are not

included in the 47-case application summary. Each reported case has one primary

group; shared coverage and retests do not increase the case count. Appendix A

provides the case register and execution conditions.


White-Box Testing

        White-box testing exercised internal functions, decision conditions and state

changes using controlled inputs and defined expected outcomes. The 38 cases

checked input preparation, frame handling, recognition interfaces, boundary

processing, sign acceptance, the Finish gesture, English-generation integration and

application data handling.
