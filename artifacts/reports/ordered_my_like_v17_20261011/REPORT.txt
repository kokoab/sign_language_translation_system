WHAT THE RULE ACTUALLY DOES
Use original timestamps and same-hand landmarks. Detect positive flat-palm/chest evidence; examine thumb-to-middle closure plus chest-relative hand departure or apparent hand-size growth. A closure blocks MY even when departure cannot be confirmed. Missing landmarks, gaps, ambiguous posture or incomplete future return unknown and preserve the model.

Only an existing candidate whose top word is LIKE and whose top three include MY can be locally relabelled MY. Original cached model scores remain intact; the local MY/LIKE scores are exchanged before decoding. Existing model MY is never changed into LIKE. These relabelled scores are heuristic evidence, not newly calibrated MY probabilities. LIKE early publication is withheld while bounded future evidence is gathered. Maximum lookahead is 0.5 seconds, cut before a decoded next word; hidden letters and MY/LIKE fragments do not count as that next word. The shoulder cache is limited to 1.5 seconds, respecting sparse auxiliary observations.

This is deliberately not “no visible pinch always means MY.” Two-dimensional departure/scale is a proxy for motion away from the body, and decoded word boundaries are fallible. A longer true LIKE can have a temporary flat-chest portion. The control set itself demonstrates why absence over an arbitrary short window cannot prove the whole sign has ended.

WHY THE REMAINING MY DISAPPEARS
The phase0 diagnostic contains a correct short MY candidate. At available time 3.167s the decoded path is MY at 2.767–2.867s. At 3.267s it becomes LIKE; at 3.5s the path is LIKE 2.8–3.0s followed by NAME 3.1–3.2s, and the motion rule correctly supplies MY evidence. At 3.8s the model/decoder instead selects a longer NAME window beginning at 2.8s. Normal publication delay means the short MY has not been committed before that replacement. This is an observed interaction between candidate windows, path selection and commitment; it is not evidence of a new model change in this experiment.

Both residual failures had temporary positive MY cues. However, so did two true LIKE control clips (LIKE_5db52de4 and LIKE_msasl_RMUydgQr9TE_5); their eventual outputs remain correct under v3. Therefore forcing every transient confirmation to commit is not justified by these results. A safe completion rule must distinguish an actual following gesture from the unfinished portion of LIKE, then protect that completed MY boundary. Simply retaining candidates, increasing their weight or committing early is not established as safe.
